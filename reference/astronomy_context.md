# Astronomy Context

## Why Astronomy Needs Statistics

Astronomers cannot usually touch, repeat, or control the systems they study.
They often work with faint light, long distances, small samples, imperfect
instruments, and rare events. Statistics helps turn those imperfect observations
into careful claims.

## Recurring Course Examples

- **Stellar brightness**: repeated measurements vary because of instrument noise,
  atmosphere, and sometimes real stellar variability.
- **Photon counts**: light arrives in discrete packets, so faint sources can look
  randomly brighter or dimmer from one exposure to the next.
- **Exoplanet transits**: a planet crossing a star causes a tiny dip in brightness
  that must be distinguished from noise.
- **Galaxy redshift**: galaxy spectra reveal motion and cosmic expansion, but
  measurements still include uncertainty.
- **Hertzsprung-Russell diagrams**: temperature and luminosity patterns show how
  stars differ and evolve.
